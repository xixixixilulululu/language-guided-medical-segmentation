recorded before the freeze (`configs/phase2_frozen/phase3_decision.json`). Their contents are not reported here.
The program also prints the rule outcome with the label "(validation)" whatever the split; on the test run that
label is wrong. It is fixed text in the frozen code and is not changed.

## 12. Interpretation and conclusions

All statements describe how the Dice of the frozen models responds to the benchmark texts given to them. They do
not show that the models "understand" or verify a clinical report, and the texts are benchmark annotations, not
independently verified reports.

### 12.1 Observed results (QaTa-COV19, 472 test units; confirmatory family of 11 per method, Holm)

1. **The paired text matters for all three models.** An empty text (C1) lowers Dice by +12.35 (DD-CMD) to
   +20.13 (LViT-T) points against the original (C0 − C1, unit-weighted, all Holm p < 0.05).
2. **A wrong report lowers Dice by more than an empty text when all three attributes are wrong.** C0 − C6 is
   +23.31 to +24.40 points, and C1 − C6 is positive for all three methods: +3.12 (LViT-T), +7.60 (RecLMIS),
   +11.39 (DD-CMD), all Holm p < 0.05. A three-attribute mismatch therefore causes harm beyond the loss of
   guidance.
3. **For one- and two-attribute mismatches, "wrong versus no text" depends on the model.** C1 − C4 is −4.30
   (LViT-T, Holm p < 0.05), +0.20 (RecLMIS, not significant) and +2.19 (DD-CMD, Holm p < 0.05); C1 − C5 is −9.09
   (LViT-T) and −2.56 (RecLMIS), both Holm p < 0.05, and +1.24 (DD-CMD, Holm p = 0.06). For LViT-T a partly wrong
   report still gives a higher Dice than an empty text.
4. **Attribute distance is not monotone.** On the common set S (2081 images, 98.5% coverage), C0 − C5 (+11.09 to
   +14.24) is smaller than C0 − C4 (+14.53 to +17.00) for all three methods; C0 − C6 is the largest. In QaTa, C4
   is almost always a location-only change (6149 of 6243 donor pairs), and the harm follows the **location**: a
   location-only change costs 12.6–13.8 points, a count-only or scope-only change 0.1–1.1 points; in C5, pairs
   that include the location cost 8.8–17.9 points, scope + count 0.1–0.8 points (descriptive). The number of
   changed attributes is therefore a poor summary of what changes for these models.
5. **Incomplete text.** Deleting the location clause (C0 − C3c) lowers Dice by +11.43 to +21.76 points for all
   three methods. Deleting the scope word (C3a) or the count (C3b) costs LViT-T +12.55 / +7.80 points but
   RecLMIS and DD-CMD only +0.36 to +1.69 points (all Holm p < 0.05).
6. **Equivalent wording (C2).** Two word substitutions change 99.0–99.8% of the QaTa masks, but the mean Dice
   change is small for LViT-T (+0.08) and DD-CMD (+0.01), both not significant. **RecLMIS loses +1.25
   [+0.95, +1.55] points** (Holm p < 0.05), as on validation (+0.99): its output depends on the exact wording,
   not only on the content.
7. **Susceptibility (Task 2.7) is similar across models.** H = C0 − C6 is 24.02 / 23.54 / 23.68 points
   (LViT-T / RecLMIS / DD-CMD, image-weighted); the paired differences are at most 1.10 points (LViT-T − RecLMIS
   −1.10 [−2.10, −0.10]; the other two pairs' CIs include 0). The models differ more in how they split H between
   losing the text (C0 − C1: 18.65 / 16.66 / 13.29) and following the wrong text (C1 − C6: 5.37 / 6.88 / 10.40).
   This describes model behaviour; it does not isolate the effect of a particular fusion module.

### 12.2 Validation and test agree for the main effects

- **QaTa:** 31 of 33 comparisons have the same sign on both splits and 30 of 33 the same Holm decision
  (validation 30, test 29 with Holm p < 0.05). C0 − C1, C0 − C3c, C0 − C4/C5/C6, C1 − C6 and RecLMIS's C0 − C2
  replicate.
- **What did not replicate** are three "wrong versus no text" comparisons for one- or two-attribute mismatches:
  RecLMIS C1 − C4 (validation −2.16, significant; test +0.20, not significant), DD-CMD C1 − C4 (+0.13, not
  significant; +2.19, significant) and DD-CMD C1 − C5 (−1.98, significant; +1.24, not significant; the largest
  validation–test difference, 3.22 points). These are close to zero and their sign is not stable; they should
  not be interpreted as a property of the model.
- Phase 2's validation findings that motivated Phase 3 (large C0 − C5 / C0 − C6 harms and positive C1 − C6 on
  QaTa) are confirmed on test.

### 12.3 Exploratory findings (MosMedData+, 9 test units; every interval and p-value exploratory)

- Mismatched text has a small effect: C0 − C4 and C0 − C5 are +0.40 to +1.35 points, C0 − C6 +0.11 to +2.95
  (unit-weighted); no CI on S excludes 0.
- The empty text gives a **lower** Dice than any mismatched report for all three methods (C1 − C6: −4.48,
  −8.23, −0.42), as on validation. RecLMIS depends most on having some text (C0 − C1 +11.19, the only C0 − C1
  with Holm p < 0.05).
- Validation and test have the same sign in 32 of 33 comparisons; 13 comparisons had Holm p < 0.05 on
  validation (11 units) and 2 on test (9 units).
- In MosMed, a count change harms more than a location change (C4 count-only 0.6–2.0 points, location-only
  −0.06 to +0.20), unlike QaTa.
- The D6 check (37 scope-conflict images removed) leaves every sign unchanged; the largest change is
  RecLMIS C1 − C5 (−10.33 to −12.92).

### 12.4 Limitations

- The texts are template-like benchmark annotations; C4–C6 swap real reports of other cases, so they test
  mismatch with these templates, not real-world reporting errors.
- C1 for LViT-T and RecLMIS is padded to tokens that resemble a "no infection" report (PHASE2_PROTOCOL.md
  section 6); C1 − Cx partly measures that.
- QaTa patient identity rests on the BIDS `sub-S` naming. MosMed has only 9 test units and a known train / val /
  test volume overlap; it is exploratory throughout.
- RecLMIS truncates some QaTa reports at 18 tokens under every condition; LViT-T sees only a 10-word window.
- Phase 1 clean (C0) test results had been seen before the freeze; the protected outcome was the counterfactual
  test evaluation.
- Three models, one checkpoint per seed: the results describe these models, not text-guided segmentation in
  general.

### 12.5 Conclusions

1. On QaTa-COV19, all three language-guided models depend strongly on the paired report: removing it costs 12–20
   Dice points, and a fully mismatched report costs 23–24 points, more than removing it. This replicates the
   validation result on the locked test split.
2. What matters most is the **location** in the report. The number of changed attributes does not order the
   harm.
3. Whether a partly wrong report is better or worse than no report depends on the model and was not stable
   between validation and test.
4. RecLMIS is sensitive to equivalent rewording (−1.25 points); LViT-T and DD-CMD are not, on average, although
   almost every mask changes.
5. On MosMedData+ (exploratory), mismatched reports have small effects and missing text is worse than a wrong
   one.
6. These results support studying a reliability-aware fallback (Phase 3, decided on validation before the
   freeze); they do not show that any model can detect a wrong report.
